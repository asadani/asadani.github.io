Filter, then rank, and where I would change the score.

Hard filters are deterministic policy, not LLM judgment: missing connection, missing scope, a write tool for a read-only request, a tool unavailable in this environment. They run before any model sees a candidate, and they are cheap to test.

The reranker then cuts roughly 30 to 50 candidates to a menu of 5 to 8. I keep the cut generous. A reranker error deletes the right tool for good, so this is the stage where I trade a few extra tokens for recall. In my paper I recommended k of 8 to 12 with the threshold doing the precision work.

A popular way to specify the reranker is a weighted sum: intent match, capability match, entity match, permission, history, minus risk, latency and cost. I like the inputs and would change how they combine.

Anything that must never happen belongs in a filter or a policy. Anything that is a preference belongs in the ranker. A weighted sum blurs the two, and the blur is where incidents come from.
