Compared with what.

During the build, I asked: “Is the same foot just sitting at two different places, or genuinely there is some distinction?”

I asked for numbers with and without the framework. The hard part was choosing “without.” A model directly triggering refunds is an easy opponent. A competent application already validates the ledger and handles uncertain execution.

The synthetic comparison used a deliberately minimal model-directed arm and two governed arms, sharing evidence, fault schedules and a simulator with its own protections. With fixture assessments, both application controls and Decision Firewall produced zero contract-violating effects and zero unresolved attempts after recovery. The minimal arm produced ninety five violating effects across one hundred and seventy request trials.

That established the value of controls. It did not distinguish the two ways of implementing them.

On the matched straightforward-success subset, application ninety fifth percentile latency latency was twenty six point five two milliseconds versus sixty seven point eight nine milliseconds for the framework. The paired median added cost was twenty three point two three milliseconds across twenty five pairs. The full run repeated thirty two frozen scenarios five times; those repetitions measured runtime variation, not independent business situations. Refund comparison.

My next question should have been about integration effort: if both enforce the same contract, does the package make that contract easier to implement and maintain?
